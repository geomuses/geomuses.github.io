# What Makes Japanese Customer Service Unique

Japan is known for its good customer service. Many people who visit the country's shops, hotels and restaurants find that workers there are polite, kind and ready to help.

The country's good service may come from something known as _omotenashi_. Often translated as "hospitality," it means working hard to make customers happy while not asking for anything back.

So, of course, that means there's no need to tip for good service in Japan!

_Omotenashi_ can be seen in the little things. Workers may bow when customers walk into their shop. Customers may get a towel to clean their hands before eating at a restaurant. Workers will speak to customers using very polite language.

Shop workers also won't be talking to each other — they'll be waiting to see what their customer needs!

Japanese also has the phrase, _okyakusama wa kamisama_ — which means, "the customer is god." And if the customer is god, it can sometimes be very difficult for businesses to say no to them!

But all these customer service rules can also make it difficult for workers to help a customer with a problem they've never seen before — because they don't know what the rule is!

Of course, good customer service can be found in many places around the world. But people who visit Japan may find that they remember the country's _omotenashi_ long after they've gone home!
