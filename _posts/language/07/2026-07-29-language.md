# What Is the 'Placebo Effect'?

In a 2014 study published in _Science Translational Medicine_, 66 people who suffered from migraines were given envelopes with pills inside.

Most envelopes were labeled correctly with either "Maxalt" — the name of a migraine medicine — or "placebo," meaning the pill was fake, and there was no actual medicine in it.

But two envelopes were mislabeled on purpose: one was labeled "Maxalt" but actually contained a placebo, and one was labeled "placebo" but contained Maxalt.

The patients reported similar reductions in pain after taking either pill.

This is an example of the "placebo effect," which happens when a person's symptoms improve even if the treatment they've been given is fake.

Placebos are "look-alike" treatments. They can be sugar pills, a saline solution injection, or some other type of fake treatment.

Medical experts have observed that the more a patient expects from a placebo, the more effective it will be. For example, studies have found that four placebo pills are better than two, and placebo injections are more powerful than pills.

Even pill colors can make a difference. For example, blue placebo pills have been found to result in stronger calming effects than pink ones.

How placebos work isn't fully understood. Some experts think our faith in the effectiveness of the placebo — which we usually don't know is fake — is enough to trigger the feel-good brain chemical dopamine and our natural painkillers, endorphins.

In 2021, Professor Ted Kaptchuk told _Harvard Health Publishing_ that placebos have been shown to be most effective for pain, cancer treatment side effects like nausea and tiredness, and even insomnia caused by stress.

However, he said, "Placebos may make you feel better, but they will not cure you."

Placebo in Latin means "I shall please." And before it referred to fake medicine, for hundreds of years the term was used to refer to flattery.

The placebo also has an evil twin called the "nocebo," which means "I will be harmful." Nocebos are also fake treatments, but instead of having a positive effect, they have a negative effect.
