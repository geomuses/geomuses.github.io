# South Korean Talent Eases Japan's Labor Shortage

High youth unemployment in South Korea and a shortage of entry-level roles are causing many young South Koreans to look for work in Japan.

In June this year, the youth unemployment rate in South Korea was 7%. Young people in South Korea are finding it hard to find entry-level jobs with major companies, as many employers prefer to hire people with experience, and a lot of older people stay in their jobs.

At the same time, Japan's aging population has resulted in a labor shortage, and the overall unemployment rate there is just 2.5%. Many Japanese companies are finding it hard to find candidates for training programs aimed at new graduates.

Such on-the-job training programs are unusual in South Korea, so they are very attractive to young Koreans as a first step into a professional career.

Data from the South Korean government shows that in 2025, almost 2,300 young Koreans took jobs in Japan through government-supported programs. This was an increase of 47% from the year before, while the number taking jobs in the US fell by 29%.

Japan is now the top destination for overseas work through these programs. Three Japanese job fairs have been held in Seoul this year, and there are a growing number of recruitment services aimed at bringing South Korean candidates to Japan.

Although most of the foreigners working in Japan are employed in manual labor or skilled trades, South Korean workers usually take on more professional jobs.

Japanese employers see young South Koreans as hard-working and value them for their Japanese language skills, as well as their ability to fit in with Japanese culture, _Reuters_ reports.

And the employment trend is just one way in which relations between the two neighboring countries are becoming closer. Marriages between South Korean and Japanese citizens are also increasing, and South Koreans are now the biggest group of foreign tourists in Japan, with a record number visiting the country this year.
